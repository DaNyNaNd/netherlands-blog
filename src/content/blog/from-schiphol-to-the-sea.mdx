By the time we made it through passport control at Schiphol, everyone was tired. The line had taken a long time, and we still had one very important traveler to find: Guppy.

We waited at the KLM help desk while an agent located her. Then Ava and I headed to oversized baggage claim, where our labradoodle was waiting in her huge kennel. We loaded the crate onto a baggage cart and rolled her out of the airport. With Guppy finally beside us, the family was together again—and we could get moving.

We divided ourselves between two vans, one carrying our mountain of luggage and the other carrying us, and set off for the Novotel Den Haag City Centre. Halfway there came the realization: my guitar was still sitting on the baggage belt at Schiphol. I submitted a Lost Item report and kept going. There was not much else to do.

After checking in, we walked around the corner to Hans im Glück for dinner. Everyone ordered burgers—go figure—and I tried fries with mayo, which were pretty good. Ava was more adventurous and ordered bitterballen, but one taste was enough for her. Then Lauren looked around and said, “You guys! We’re in Europe!” Afterward, we went back to the hotel and crashed.

## House Day, with a beach trip

The next morning was House Day: the day we got our keys and watched our IKEA delivery arrive. The house was officially ours, even though our lives were still packed into bags and boxes and we would spend one more night at the hotel.

## The house starts to become ours

Settling in quickly divided us into two teams. Lauren and the kids went to a playground, got ice cream, and bought groceries. I stayed at the house and assembled IKEA furniture. Piece by piece, the stacks of flat-packed boxes began turning into things we could actually use.

Our first morning in the house brought a practical surprise: the kitchen had an induction cooktop, and our fully stainless American pans would not work with it. So breakfast moved elsewhere. Afterward, we stopped at Albert Heijn for more groceries—and, importantly, coffee for me—then returned to the house to work on immigration paperwork.

Later, we set the paperwork aside and walked toward the beach. From our side of Den Haag, Westduinpark is a straight shot to the sea. Halfway there, Ava found her happy place: horses in a ring beside the route. Then we came over the rise and saw the beach from our neighborhood for the first time.

It was chilly and very windy, but the walk kept offering reasons to stop. A fox wandered through the dune grass while we watched, and several jellyfish had washed onto the sand. Scheveningen had felt like a beach outing. This stretch of coast was different: it was connected directly to the neighborhood where we lived.

## The things we went back for

We began the next day with bacon and eggs—an American breakfast in our new Dutch kitchen—and then the whole family retraced part of our arrival route. We took the tram and train back to Schiphol to retrieve my guitar.

Getting it back was an ordeal. Security let me into baggage claim, which was surprising, and the KLM desk helped us work out where to go. Eventually, after the extra journey and plenty of airport navigation, the guitar was back in our hands.

## The first ordinary days

After the airport adventure, we were ready for a quieter day. Lauren and I went to HEMA while the kids stayed home, and later we took a walk around the neighborhood. Then I picked up Ava’s bike and rode it home—my first bike ride in the Netherlands. I was nervous at the start, but I ended up doing pretty well.

That night brought another first: ordering dinner through Thuisbezorgd, the Dutch equivalent of DoorDash.

The final day was quieter still. I finished assembling the IKEA furniture, we sorted out all the recycling, and Ava took her bike for a ride. Nothing about the day was dramatic, which was exactly what made it feel like progress.

Seven days after landing, the move was still not tidy, but it had begun to make sense. We had our keys, Guppy, a recovered guitar, and a beach that ran in a straight line from the neighborhood. Around those landmarks, the beginnings of a routine were taking shape: trams, groceries, bikes, recycling, and the ongoing question of what we could cook on an induction stove.

Somewhere between rolling Guppy through Schiphol and watching Ava try her bike, the Netherlands stopped being only the place we had moved to. It started becoming the place where our days happened.
